Arshdeep × Meenurainy little goodbye ↓
DATE EIGHT · 14 AUGUST 2026

A little rain before you had to leave.

It was supposed to be a small meeting before you went home from the hostel. Instead, the rain arrived almost as soon as we did — and somehow made those few hours feel even more ours.

the sweetest surprise

You brought me firni.

You had brought a little sweet for me, and I still love that detail because it was such a simple way of saying you had thought about me before we even met.

Then came kulcha, a shopkeeper's umbrella, and rain falling around us while we stood there trying to stay dry.

and then we ran through the rain

We got drenched anyway.

We walked hastily back toward the bus station, getting wetter with every step. It wasn't polished or planned or particularly comfortable.

But I think that's exactly why I want to remember it. You were beside me, the rain was everywhere, and we were just trying to make it back in time.

a tiny detail that makes me laugh

I bought you Lahori Jeera without knowing you don't like it.

There are probably grander romantic gestures, but this one feels very us. I bought it without knowing that you actually don't like Lahori Jeera.

And then, at the station, we met one of your college classmates and talked for a little longer before it was finally time for your bus.

the goodbye

Then your bus came.

You left for your village, and I got onto my own bus back toward college. It was a short meeting, but I don't think the length of a day decides how much of it stays with you.

I keep coming back to the little pieces: the firni you brought, the kulcha, the umbrella, the rain, the silly Lahori Jeera, and those last minutes before you left.

the detail I want to keep

Even a goodbye can become a memory worth keeping.

14 August wasn't a grand date. It was just a little bit of time before you went home. But you were there, you brought something for me, we ate together, laughed through the rain, and tried to steal a few more minutes before the buses took us in different directions.

Sometimes the smallest meetings become the ones you remember most clearly.
